Taxes for Military Families in Montana

Montana has no state sales tax, and active-duty pay earned by Montana residents serving on active duty is generally exempt from state income tax. Military retirement pay has historically been taxed, though recent legislation created a partial exemption for retirees who establish Montana residency — check current rules with the Department of Revenue. Property taxes are moderate, and disabled veterans meeting income limits can qualify for a significant property tax reduction on their primary residence.

Montana Veteran Benefits Worth Knowing

  • Disabled American Veteran property tax assistance program reduces property taxes for qualifying 100% disabled veterans and surviving spouses, subject to income limits
  • Partial state income tax exemption available for military retirement pay for qualifying new residents
  • Montana University System honorably discharged veteran tuition waiver for eligible wartime veterans
  • Free or reduced-fee hunting and fishing licenses and state park access for disabled veterans

The Montana Housing Market for Military Buyers

Great Falls is one of Montana's most affordable metros, a world apart from Bozeman or Missoula pricing. Median home prices near Malmstrom typically sit below the national average, and an E-5's BAH often covers the payment on a solid single-family home. Inventory can be thin in winter months, so buyers PCSing in summer face the most competition. Helena, near Fort Harrison, runs somewhat higher but remains reasonable by mountain-west standards.

Top School Districts & Counties in Montana

  • Near Malmstrom AFB: Great Falls Public Schools (Cascade County) serves most base families and offers strong military student support
  • Cascade County suburbs: Belt and Centerville districts draw families wanting small-town schools within commuting range
  • Near Fort Harrison: Helena Public Schools (Lewis and Clark County) is consistently among Montana's better-regarded districts
  • East Helena Public Schools: a growing district with newer housing developments popular with Guard families

Weather & Climate in Montana

Great Falls winters are long and cold, with stretches below 0°F and sudden chinook warm-ups that stress roofs and pipes. Buyers should prioritize newer windows, quality insulation, and a well-maintained furnace — heating costs are a real line item. Snow load matters for roofs, and gravel roads in outlying subdivisions can drift shut. Summers are dry and pleasant, but wildfire smoke is increasingly common in August, and homes bordering grassland should have defensible space.

Traffic & Commutes in Montana

Traffic is a minor concern in Montana. The commute from most Great Falls neighborhoods to Malmstrom's main gate off 2nd Avenue North runs 10-20 minutes; east-side neighborhoods like Skyline and Highland Park are closest. Winter driving is the real variable — black ice on 10th Avenue South and drifting on rural routes add time from December through March. In Helena, Fort Harrison sits on the west edge of town, and even cross-town commutes rarely exceed 20 minutes.
