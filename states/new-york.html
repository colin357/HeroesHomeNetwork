PCSing to New York

Military pensions are fully exempt from state income tax, and upstate markets near Fort Drum are strikingly affordable.

Historic academy on the Hudson, with faculty and staff buying in pricey but commutable Orange County towns.

Taxes for Military Families in New York

New York fully exempts military pensions from state income tax, along with SBP payments. Active-duty pay is taxable for New York residents, but service members domiciled elsewhere pay no New York tax on military pay while stationed in the state — and New York residents stationed outside the state for the full year may qualify for nonresident treatment. Property taxes are high, especially downstate, but the Alternative Veterans' Exemption reduces assessed value for combat and wartime veterans in most participating localities.

New York Veteran Benefits Worth Knowing

  • Full state income tax exemption on military retirement pay and SBP
  • Alternative Veterans' Property Tax Exemption reducing assessed value for wartime and combat veterans, with an added benefit for service-connected disability (adoption varies by locality)
  • Veterans Tuition Awards covering tuition at SUNY rates for eligible combat veterans
  • MERIT scholarship and hiring credits, plus free state park access for certain disabled veterans

The New York Housing Market for Military Buyers

Forget Manhattan math — the markets around New York's installations are mostly upstate and affordable. Jefferson County around Fort Drum has median prices far below the national average, and BAH easily carries a mortgage in Watertown, though winters and deployment cycles make some soldiers prefer renting. West Point is the outlier: Orange County prices and property taxes are substantial. Saratoga Springs is a strong, appreciating market, and Syracuse near Hancock Field is one of the cheapest metros in the Northeast.

Top School Districts & Counties in New York

  • Near Fort Drum: Indian River Central School District serves most base families and receives significant Impact Aid; Watertown City and General Brown are also common picks (Jefferson County)
  • Near West Point: Cornwall and Monroe-Woodbury in Orange County are perennial faculty favorites
  • Near NSA Saratoga Springs: Saratoga Springs City and Shenendehowa (Saratoga County) rank among upstate's best districts
  • Near Hancock Field: Fayetteville-Manlius and Jamesville-DeWitt (Onondaga County) lead the Syracuse suburbs

Weather & Climate in New York

Fort Drum sits in the Tug Hill lake-effect snow belt — Watertown-area homes see well over 100 inches of snow in a normal winter, so buyers should scrutinize roof condition and snow load, insulation, heated or short driveways, and heating systems (many rural homes burn fuel oil or propane). Temperatures below 0°F are routine in January. West Point and Saratoga winters are milder but still real. Spring melt raises basement-water questions everywhere upstate; a working sump pump is standard equipment.

Traffic & Commutes in New York

Around Fort Drum, distance is measured in snow, not traffic: Route 11 and I-81 from Watertown take 15-25 minutes on clear days but can crawl in lake-effect whiteouts, which is why many families buy close in Evans Mills, LeRay, or Calcium. West Point commuters face Route 9W and Route 293 chokepoints plus gate queues, and anything across the Hudson adds bridge traffic. Saratoga and Syracuse commutes are easy, though I-87 Northway congestion builds south of Saratoga Springs.
