PCSing to Utah

A strong economy, top-rated schools, and a growing set of tax breaks for military retirees.

Duty Stations

Major installations in Utah

Major Air Force logistics and F-35 base along the Wasatch Front, where Davis and Weber County suburbs compete for military buyers.

Taxes for Military Families in Utah

Utah taxes income at a single flat rate near 4.5%, but offers a nonrefundable tax credit that effectively eliminates state tax on military retirement pay. Active-duty pay for nonresidents stationed in Utah is not taxed. Property taxes are below the national average, and veterans with a disability rating can claim a property tax abatement that scales with their rating percentage.

Utah Veteran Benefits Worth Knowing

  • State tax credit that offsets tax on military retirement pay
  • Property tax abatement for disabled veterans, scaled to the VA rating
  • Purple Heart recipients receive tuition waivers at state schools
  • Veterans preference in state employment and free recreation passes for qualifying disabled veterans

The Utah Housing Market for Military Buyers

The Wasatch Front is one of the pricier duty-station markets in the interior West: Davis County suburbs like Layton, Clearfield, and Syracuse near Hill AFB commonly price above the national median, and BAH math is tighter than in most Air Force towns. Weber County (Roy, Ogden) offers meaningfully lower entry points a few minutes farther out. Strong job growth and chronic undersupply support long-term appreciation, making buy-and-hold a common strategy for PCSing families.

Top School Districts & Counties in Utah

  • Near Hill AFB: Davis School District (Layton, Syracuse, Kaysville) is the dominant choice for base families
  • Weber County: Weber School District serves Roy and the more affordable neighborhoods just west of the base
  • Near Camp Williams: Jordan and Alpine districts cover the fast-growing Bluffdale, Herriman, and Lehi corridor
  • Near Dugway: Tooele County School District serves Tooele and Stansbury Park, the main commuter towns

Weather & Climate in Utah

Utah is a four-season, low-humidity state. Wasatch Front winters bring regular snow — check driveway grade, roof condition, and whether the neighborhood sits in a heavier lake-effect snow band near Ogden. Summers are dry with highs in the 90s°F, and secondary water or irrigation shares affect landscaping costs in many Davis County neighborhoods. Winter temperature inversions trap air pollution in the valleys, which some buyers weigh against bench-elevation homes. Seismic risk along the Wasatch Fault makes earthquake insurance worth a conversation.

Traffic & Commutes in Utah

I-15 is the spine and the bottleneck. Hill AFB commuters from Layton, Clearfield, and Roy have short gate runs, but anyone commuting from Salt Lake City fights 30-plus minutes of I-15 congestion each way; the FrontRunner commuter rail to Clearfield is a genuine alternative. Around Camp Williams, the Bluffdale and Herriman growth corridor jams at Bangerter Highway and Mountain View Corridor during rush hour. Dugway families in Tooele face a long but uncrowded drive via SR-36.
