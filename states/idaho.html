PCS State Guide

PCSing to Idaho

Mountain-West quality of life with growing markets and meaningful property tax relief for disabled veterans.

Duty Stations

Major installations in Idaho

Mountain Home AFB (Mountain Home)

Fighter-wing base about 50 miles from Boise; the town of Mountain Home itself offers some of the cheapest home prices of any Air Force assignment.

Gowen Field (Boise)

Idaho Air and Army National Guard hub on the edge of Boise, putting members in one of the fastest-appreciating housing markets in the West.

Idaho Falls Naval Reactors Facility (Idaho Falls)

Nuclear-power training billets in eastern Idaho, where median home prices sit well below Boise and the national average.

Taxes for Military Families in Idaho

Idaho has a flat state income tax, but active-duty pay earned outside Idaho by residents is exempt, and nonresidents stationed in Idaho don't pay Idaho tax on military pay. Military retirement pay is deductible for retirees age 65 and older (62 if disabled). VA disability compensation is untaxed. Disabled veterans may qualify for a property tax reduction of up to $1,500 on their primary residence, on top of Idaho's circuit-breaker program.

Idaho Veteran Benefits Worth Knowing

  • Property tax reduction of up to $1,500 per year for veterans with a 100% service-connected disability rating
  • Military retirement pay deduction for retirees 65+ (62 if disabled)
  • Idaho State Veterans Homes and a dedicated Veterans Recognition Fund for education and emergency grants
  • Free or reduced-fee hunting and fishing licenses for disabled veterans

The Idaho Housing Market for Military Buyers

Idaho is a tale of two markets. The city of Mountain Home remains genuinely affordable — an E-5's BAH goes a long way on a single-family home — while Boise and the Treasure Valley have appreciated sharply, so Gowen Field families often look to Kuna, Nampa, or Caldwell to keep payments inside BAH. Inventory is tighter than the national average, so getting pre-approved on your VA loan before house hunting matters here.

Top School Districts & Counties in Idaho

  • Near Mountain Home AFB: Mountain Home School District 193 serves base families directly and runs on-base support programs
  • Near Gowen Field: West Ada School District (Meridian) is Idaho's largest and a consistent favorite for military families
  • Boise School District: strong established neighborhoods with short commutes to Gowen Field
  • Kuna School District (Ada County): newer construction and lower price points within commuting range of Boise-area duty

Weather & Climate in Idaho

Southern Idaho is high desert: hot, dry summers around 90-100°F and cold winters with regular snow, though valley snowfall is manageable compared to the mountains. For buyers, that means checking insulation quality and heating systems (winter utility bills are real), and confirming irrigation rights for landscaping in this arid climate. Hail and severe-storm roof damage is far less common than in the Plains, and hurricane or flood insurance is rarely a factor outside mapped river floodplains near the Boise and Snake rivers.

Traffic & Commutes in Idaho

Mountain Home has essentially no traffic — the trade-off is the roughly 45-minute run up I-84 if you choose to live in Boise, a commute some airmen accept for the amenities. Around Gowen Field, I-84 through Meridian and Nampa backs up at rush hour as the Treasure Valley grows, so living in southeast Boise or Kuna saves meaningful time versus Caldwell. Winter driving on I-84 can be icy; garage space and all-wheel drive are worth weighing when you buy.

Buying in Idaho with a VA loan: $0 down and no PMI apply everywhere in the state.
