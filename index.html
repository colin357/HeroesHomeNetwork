Why Not a Big Bank

Why this beats a big bank or a call-center VA lender.

At a national bank or a call-center VA lender, your file is a ticket in a queue. You explain BAH to one rep, upload your LES for a second, and by the time an underwriter asks why your Leave and Earnings Statement shows a housing allowance that isn't on your W-2, you're on your fourth point of contact and nobody owns your report date. Their agent referral desk, if they have one, hands you whoever paid for the ZIP code that month — often someone who has never written a VA offer and will quietly steer you toward conventional financing because it's easier for them. Heroes Home Network works the other way: one in-house VA lender who underwrites military income every day, one pre-vetted agent who has PCS'd or been the spouse holding the household together during one, and a single group chat where your closing date and your report date are the same conversation. When a listing agent pushes back on a VA offer, your agent has answered that objection before. When a rate lock needs to move because your orders moved, you text the person who can move it — not a 1-800 number.

The Vetting Standard

How an agent earns a spot in the network.

Location match is the starting point, not the standard. Every agent we introduce you to has cleared all of the following before they ever get your name.

  • Military connection, verified. Veteran, current service member, or military spouse — confirmed with a DD-214, current orders, or a dependent ID before onboarding.
  • Closing volume, documented. A minimum of 12 closed transactions in the last 24 months, with at least 4 of them financed with a VA loan — verified against MLS records or closing statements, not self-reported.
  • Reviews that hold up. A 4.7-or-better average across at least 15 verified client reviews, read individually. A pattern of complaints about communication or responsiveness is disqualifying on its own.
  • Three reference checks by phone. One past military client, one lender or title partner, and one managing broker. We ask specifically about responsiveness during a compressed PCS timeline and about how they handled a deal that went sideways.
  • License in good standing. Active license verified with the state commission, with no unresolved disciplinary action or pending complaints.
  • VA-loan literacy, tested. Completion of our VA loan class and a working command of appraisal requirements, the Tidewater process, escape clauses, funding fees, and what a seller can and cannot be asked to pay.
  • Limited seats per market. We cap how many agents we accept around each installation so the location match means one accountable name, not a rotating roster — and we re-check closings, reviews, and standing every 12 months to keep it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers for military home buyers.

Can I buy a house while PCSing?

Yes. Many military families get pre-approved for a VA loan as soon as they receive orders, shop remotely or during a house-hunting trip, and close shortly after arriving. A VA loan specialist familiar with PCS timelines can align your closing date with your report date.

How much house can I afford on BAH?

BAH is designed to cover housing costs at your duty station, and lenders count it as qualifying income. Use our BAH calculator to estimate your allowance, then talk to a lender about how it translates into a mortgage budget.

Do I need a down payment for a VA loan?

No. The VA loan allows qualified veterans and service members to buy with $0 down and no private mortgage insurance (PMI), which typically saves hundreds of dollars per month compared to conventional financing.

How do you pick the agent I get matched with?

By location first — we match you to an agent who works the market around your next duty station, not a national call center. But location is only the entry requirement. Every agent in the network is a veteran, service member, or military spouse with verified closing volume (12+ closings in 24 months, at least 4 of them VA-financed), a 4.7+ review average across 15 or more verified reviews, three completed phone reference checks, and a license in good standing. See the full vetting standard.

When do I actually meet my agent?

Right after pre-approval. We introduce you within one business day of your approval and open a group chat with you, your agent, and your lender in it — so your house hunt, your loan, and your report date are all one conversation instead of three.

Can I use my VA loan more than once?

Yes. VA loan entitlement is reusable. You can restore it after selling a home, and in many cases you can even have two VA loans at once — common for families who keep a home at their old duty station as a rental.
